# AI Use and Development Transparency

**Status:** Public project disclosure  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-05

## Why we are disclosing this

This project is currently using AI-assisted tools during its early design and proof-of-concept stage. We believe people evaluating, using, or contributing to an open-source project should be able to understand how it is being developed, where AI is involved, and what role we intend AI to have in the finished software.

We do not want AI use to be hidden, overstated, or confused with a dependency of the controller itself.

## How AI is currently used

During the current architecture and proof-of-concept phase, AI tools are used as an assistant to help with work such as:

- Exploring and documenting the overall architecture.
- Drafting and refining design specifications, interfaces, and technical documentation.
- Comparing implementation approaches and identifying design trade-offs.
- Helping prototype ideas and reason through workflows.
- Reviewing configuration concepts, troubleshooting approaches, and potential failure cases.
- Supporting maintainers as they plan and organize development work.

AI-assisted material is a starting point for human review, not an automatic source of truth. The maintainers remain responsible for decisions, repository changes, correctness, security, licensing, and project direction.

This disclosure describes the current development approach. It does not mean every file, line of code, or contribution in this repository was generated by AI.

## AI is not intended to be a runtime requirement

The goal is a conventional, independently operable game-server controller. The controller's normal operation should not require:

- An AI model or model provider.
- An AI API key or external AI service.
- A subscription to an AI product.
- Network access to an AI service for core lifecycle operations.

Provisioning, backups, restores, health checks, updates, staging, routing, and rollback should be implemented as deterministic, testable software using documented interfaces and explicit operator policy.

AI may be useful during development, but it should not become an undisclosed operational dependency.

## We welcome replacing AI-assisted work

We are open to reworking, simplifying, or replacing AI-assisted designs and implementations with independently developed, conventional code.

In fact, we expect some proof-of-concept material to change substantially as the architecture is tested and reviewed. If an AI-assisted approach is unnecessarily complex, difficult to maintain, insecure, or poorly understood, we would rather replace it than preserve it merely because it was generated quickly.

We welcome contributors who want to:
- Review and challenge AI-assisted architecture or code.
- Rewrite components using simpler or more maintainable approaches.
- Replace prototypes with well-tested implementations.
- Identify unnecessary abstractions, dependencies, or complexity.
- Improve documentation so design decisions can be understood without relying on an AI tool.

The objective is a sound, maintainable open-source project—not maximizing the amount of AI used.

## Human review and accountability

AI output must not be treated as verified simply because it appears plausible or passes a superficial review.

Before AI-assisted code or designs are accepted into the official project, they must receive appropriate human review and validation. Depending on the change, this includes:

- Understanding the behavior and being able to maintain it.
- Reviewing security, privacy, licensing, and dependency implications.
- Adding or updating automated tests.
- Testing failure paths and destructive-operation safeguards.
- Verifying compatibility and documenting limitations.
- Ensuring no secrets, private data, or unapproved third-party material are introduced.

AI tools do not have maintainer authority and do not approve, merge, or release their own output.

## Contributors and AI

Contributors are not required to use AI tools. Contributions written without AI assistance are equally welcome.

If AI materially assisted a contribution, contributors are encouraged to disclose that assistance in the pull request description, including which parts were assisted and what review/testing was performed. This is intended to support transparency, not to stigmatize AI-assisted work or replace normal code review.

All contributions remain subject to the project's licensing, security, testing, and review requirements in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Our commitment

We will keep this disclosure reasonably current as our development practices change. If AI use expands beyond the current design and proof-of-concept assistance, or if an AI service ever becomes part of an optional or required product feature, that change should be clearly documented before it is introduced.

**In short:** AI is currently helping us explore and build the proof of concept. The product we want to deliver is an open, understandable, independently operable software project, and we are fully open to replacing AI-assisted work with better human-designed and tested implementations.
